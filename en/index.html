03

Experiment: AI in a language I don’t know

Can a PHP developer who knows the principles of good code build a working project in Python or C++ by relying on AI?

My answer after two projects: yes, because architecture, tests and readability are the same in every language. I own the requirements, structure and tests; AI writes the code.

The language stops being a barrier. One thing remains: you need to know the language’s strengths and weaknesses to know what you can and can’t demand from AI. The type of application matters more than the language. I build web apps every day. A desktop app – like komunikator – has different requirements: audio devices, system installation, running without a browser. That’s where I learn the most.

And someone who doesn’t code at all? They can build a simple website or a small app too – depending on the AI they use and the size of the project. At first it usually works. The trouble comes later, and it’s nobody’s fault: an author who doesn’t understand what was built can’t give the AI good context a month later. Without context, AI sometimes gets it right and sometimes breaks things. Every new piece adds more to keep track of – and the risk that everything falls apart grows.

So the line is clear: the bigger the project, the more you need to understand what you’re building. szukaj is small and finished. komunikator has a lot of room to grow, so this test is still going on.
